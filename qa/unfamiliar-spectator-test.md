# Quantum Royale V2.1 unfamiliar-spectator test

**Status:** prepared, not yet conducted
**Purpose:** test comprehension and sound discrimination; do not use the session to rebalance roles or validate quantum advantage

## Setup

1. Use one participant who has not seen Quantum Royale and has not read its technical explanation.
2. Open the current local build at the default seed. Keep LAB collapsed and sound at `0.35` unless the participant requests a different comfortable volume.
3. Say only: “This is an autonomous chicken battle. Please watch it and tell me what you think is happening. You can change speed and bet between rounds.”
4. Run at genuine `1×` through the first intermission. Do not define X/Y/Z, matched/split, roles, or quantum-derived data while they watch.
5. Then open seed `5` and run through the Round 3 intermission, where the documented role replacements appear. Briefly show `2×` and `4×` only after the `1×` observation.
6. Record the participant's wording verbatim where possible. Do not repair an answer during questioning.

## Locked questions

Ask in this order:

1. Which two chickens are the presenters discussing right now?
2. Was that event combat, support, or movement? What on screen or in the sound made you think so?
3. What repeated tendency have you noticed between any pair of chickens?
4. Did any chicken's role or identity emerge or change? Which one?
5. What did the quantum-derived distribution do in the game?
6. What parts of the battle remained classical?

## Evidence standard

- Count a relationship event as legible only if the participant identifies both the pair and the action family without coaching.
- Count a tendency only if the participant names a repeated action or consequence involving a specific pair; a generic claim such as “they fight” is insufficient.
- Count a role migration only if the participant distinguishes a changed identity from ordinary damage, score, or position.
- Count the quantum/classical boundary only if the participant says, in their own terms, that stored pair probabilities condition a joint action while the browser samples the draw and classical rules apply movement, damage, roles, betting, and display.
- Treat hesitation, misidentification, silence, and requests for explanation as evidence. Do not convert them into success through post hoc paraphrase.
- Do not alter role weights, thresholds, fixtures, or branch rules during this test. File any proposed changes as a separate follow-up.
